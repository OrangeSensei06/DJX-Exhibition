### Interactive ingredient copy

Heading: `What made this bowl rich`

| Ingredient | Current description |
|---|---|
| Rice | Rice absorbed stock, fat and spices. Regional biryanis still use different aromatic grains, giving each its own texture. |
| Saffron | Saffron brought golden colour and fragrance. Some modern recipes use colouring, recreating the appearance without the same aroma. |
| Meat | Cooking meat with rice brought their flavours together. Mughal manuscripts also record fish and paneer preparations: variation already belonged to the tradition. |
| Onions | Browning onions developed sweetness and deeper flavour. This familiar foundation remains in many biryanis today. |
| Spices | Cinnamon, cloves, cardamom and cumin built layers of fragrance. Modern recipes often combine these whole spices with ground masalas and chilli. |
| Herbs | Fresh herbs add fragrance. Mint and coriander are familiar today, although their exact match to this historical recipe is uncertain. |
| Nuts and dried fruit | Some courtly rice dishes included almonds, pistachios and raisins for contrasting textures and sweetness. Today, garnishes vary between recipes. |
| Ghee | Ghee added buttery richness between the layers. It remains in use, while some modern preparations use oil or a mixture of both. |
